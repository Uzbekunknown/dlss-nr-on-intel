# Players' reports: what NR costs on each GPU, and where a frame's time goes (2026-11-03)

Every report on GitHub up to 2026-11-03, issues #10 to #34: eleven GPU reports from
`nr_gpu_probe.exe`, covering twelve GPUs, four speed tests, three daemon logs, two screenshots of the setup window
running a game, and the frame rates players wrote down. The numbers are in
`notes/measurements/players.json`. `src/tools/player_reports.py` read them out of the reports
attached to the issues: `digest` for a Save report, `probe` for a GPU report, and `fit` for the
fits below. The attachments are not committed, because they carry the players' folders, Windows
user names and process ids.

**Measured** means a number from a report's own record: the speed test, the daemon's log, the
probe, or the setup window's status line in a screenshot. **Said** means a player's figure in
the text, usually read off an overlay. **Inferred** says what it rests on.

## The GPUs

The probe's own rates, TFLOP/s, float16 into float32: NR's shape (8x16x16) at 32 lanes, which is
how NR's kernels run, and the best rate any subgroup size reached.

| GPU | reports | NR's shape at 32 lanes | best | vector FMA at 32 lanes |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Arc Pro B70 | #30 | 141.9 | 141.9 at 32 | 18.0 |
| Arc B580 | #34, #36 (added 11-04) | 58.3-58.6 | 58.3-58.6 at 32 | 7.2 |
| Arc A770 16GB | #22-#26, #29 | 38.0-41.0 | 63.9-68.9 at 8 | 18.1-19.4 |
| Arc A770 16GB | #28, #31 | 30.8-30.9 | 51.8-51.9 at 8 | 17.7-17.8 |
| Arc A750 | #32, #33 | 26.6-26.7 | 44.7-44.8 at 8 | 15.6 |
| Arc A380 6GB | #30 | 8.0 | 13.6 at 8 | 3.8 |
| Arc 140V (this laptop, Windows) | HANDOFF archive, 10-31 evening | 28.1-30.8 | 28.1-30.8 at 16 or 32 | 3.4-3.7 |

- **All eleven A-series cards compute NR's shape exactly**, though none offers it. Each offers
  float16 8x8x16 and int8 8x8x32 only, subgroups 8 to 32, and 48 KB of shared memory. At 32
  lanes each loses about 40 % (39-42 %) of its rate at 8.
- **The Arc Pro B70 offers NR's shape natively**: the same four configurations as this laptop's
  Arc 140V under Intel's driver (phase71), and "run here". At 142 TFLOP/s it is 4.6-5x this laptop. Nobody has run NR on one yet.
- **Two Arc B580s, added 11-04** (a comment on #34 that came after this note's data, and #36, both
  on 101.9150): the same four configurations natively, "run here", and 58.3-58.6 TFLOP/s, 1.9-2.1x
  this laptop. That is 1.4-1.5x the six faster A770s at 32 lanes, less than the B580's lead in the
  network (below).
- Two of the eight A770s (#28, #31) measure 19-25 % below the other six in NR's shape at
  every lane count, but only 2-9 % below on vector work, so it is not the clock alone.
  The driver is not it either: both run 101.9150, as five of the six faster cards do. A lower
  power limit would act like this; nothing here shows one.

## NR's own speed on each GPU

The setup window's speed test (`src/bench/speed_test.py`) hands the daemon synthetic frames back
to back. Its network time is the middle of the daemon's `gpu in+run+out` split. Fitted over the
fields up to 0.45 MP (320x320 to 832x512), where games run, the network's time is a fixed part
plus a part per megapixel of network field (`player_reports.py fit`):

| GPU | network | report | fixed | per megapixel | fixed per pass | fixed part at 512x320 |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Arc B580 | A | #17, 0.0.1.2 | 4.2 ms | 36.0 ms | 10 us | 42 % |
| Arc B580 | A | #17, 0.0.1.3 | 3.7 ms | 39.5 ms | 9 us | 37 % |
| Arc A770 | A (inferred) | #23, 0.0.2 or 0.0.3 | 22.0 ms | 90.9 ms | 53 us | 60 % |
| Arc A770 | B | #25, 0.0.3.1 | 10.9 ms | 51.0 ms | 61 us | 57 % |
| Arc 140V, Windows | A | phase72 | 10.7 ms | 144.1 ms | 26 us | 31 % |
| Arc 140V, Windows | B | phase72 | 4.1 ms | 76.0 ms | 23 us | 25 % |

The worst residual is 1.9 ms. A frame runs about 415 passes on network A and 179 on network B
(`test_resident.py`, phase72).

- **On the A770 the fixed part is a cost per pass, about 55 us each.** Network B's fixed part
  is about half of A's, as its pass count is (179 against 415; its blocks, 31 against 71, are in
  the same proportion, so per block reads as well as per pass). Yet B keeps A's eight-block
  bottleneck whole (phase72), so this is not the bottleneck's work, which would be the same
  in both. It is no CPU cost either: a frame is one submission of a recorded graph
  (phase28), and the daemon times its replay. It is on the GPU, between or inside passes.
  The B580 pays 9-10 us a pass, and this laptop 23-26 us.
- **So the A770 is fast per pixel and slow per frame.** Per megapixel it needs 0.6 of this
  laptop's time on A and 0.7 on B, and at 1920x1080 and full scale it takes 229 ms against
  381-404. At 1280x720 and 0.4, where games are played, its 22 ms of passes eat the lead.
  There it takes 35 ms against this laptop's 32-34 on A, and 19 against 16 on B. In a game
  the two end level: Nuclear Option on the A770 ran 24 ms a frame on B and 42 on A, and
  Mortal Kombat 11 on this laptop 24 and 39-41 (phase72).
- **#23 measured network A.** Its report names no network (`"preset": null`, from 0.0.2 or
  0.0.3). Every
  case took 1.8-2.0 times network B's time on #25's A770. Network A takes 1.9-2.2 times B's
  on this laptop (phase72), and cards of one model differ by up to 1.3x in the probe.
- The B580 is the fastest GPU measured so far. At 1280x720 and 0.4 it takes 9 ms for
  network A, 3.9x faster than an A770, and at 1920x1080 and full scale 87-88 ms, 2.6x
  faster. Nobody has run a speed test on a B580 with network B.

**The test's round trip is not what a game waits for any more.** The test starts the daemon
without any `NR_` setting, so the frames go through the CPU. Since 0.0.2 the Windows setup
window keeps a game's frames on the GPU (`NR_GPU_IMAGE=1`). The round trip's rest, round trip minus network, is the CPU path's host passes and
copies, and it grows with the window:

| GPU, CPU | rest at 640x360, 0.4 | 1920x1080, 0.4 | 1920x1080, 1.0 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Arc B580, Core i3-12100 | 2.8-3.4 ms | 21.2-21.8 ms | 42.7-43.5 ms |
| Arc A770, Ryzen 5 5600 (#25) | 3.1 ms | 18.3 ms | 35.8 ms |
| Arc 140V (Windows) | 3-4 ms | 14-15 ms | 25 ms |

On the GPU path, the passes around the network cost 5 ms at 1280x720 on the A770 (below). So at
1920x1080 the test's result reads 10-20 ms worse than what a game waits for. DOOM on the B580
at 1920x1080 and 0.4 reached 48 fps with network A, a 21 ms frame, where the test's round trip
says 39 ms, or 26 fps.

## Where a game's frame goes

NR works in step with the game. The game draws its frame, NR's layer captures it, the daemon
runs NR, and only then does the frame go on. A frame therefore costs the game's own time plus
NR's, and in every report NR's time was most of it.

**On the GPU path (the Windows release's default since 0.0.2).** #25's log has 376 frames of Nuclear Option in
Vulkan on the A770, 1280x720 at 0.4, network A. Each frame took 42 ms in the daemon:

| part | ms | share |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| the network's fixed part: 415 passes at ~53 us | ~22 | 52 % |
| the network's part per pixel (512x320) | ~15 | 36 % |
| preparing the network's input (`prep`) | 1.3 | 3 % |
| composing the answer (`compose`) | 0.5 | 1 % |
| the rest: history copies, statistics, the copy into the game's image, fences | 3.2 | 8 % |

The game ran at 23 fps, a 43.5 ms frame, so the game's own work added only a millisecond or two. The same game
in DirectX 11, in the player's screenshot of 10-31, took 24 ms a frame in the daemon at 40 fps
(37-40 on the overlay, GPU 96 % busy). 24 ms is network B's 19 plus the same 5 ms around it, and
network A's 35-37 cannot fit in it. **So the player's "Vulkan 23 fps against DirectX 11 40"
is network A against network B (inferred).** The player also said A and B made no
difference, and that disagrees with both records; a choice made in the setup window applies
only from the game's next start. Not asked yet.

On a B580 at 1920x1080 the network takes 22 ms a frame at 0.5 (both of #17's logs). Driving
Rogue ran at 34 fps with network A, a 29.4 ms frame, so the network was about 75 % of it, and
NR's passes around it and the game's own frame shared the remaining 7 ms. With network B it ran
at 53 fps. DOOM at 0.4 ran at 73 fps on B, a 13.7 ms frame, and at 48 on A, a 20.8 ms frame,
whose network takes 17-18 ms. DOOM itself runs at its 200 fps cap without NR.

**On the CPU path** (frames off the GPU, the only path before 0.0.1.2). #17's logs at 1920x1080
and 0.5: 60 ms a frame, of which the network 22, the upload 2 and the readback 1. The remaining
35 ms, 58 %, are the host passes and the frame's two trips through the socket on a four-core
i3-12100, and the GPU sits at 46-54 %. Keeping the frames on the GPU took Driving Rogue from 16
to 34 fps on network A and from 19 to 53 on B, and DOOM at 0.4 on B from 25 to 73.

**Inside the network** (this laptop's per-pass profiles on Linux; nothing like them exists for
another GPU):

- network A at 320x320: GEMMs 16.6 ms of 24.5 of device time, of which the bottleneck's four
  GEMMs 4.2 (HANDOFF archive, 09-27, night);
- network B at 320x320: 11.6 ms; the bottleneck 3.6 (31 %), the two full-resolution blocks 2.4
  (21 %), the sixteen blocks between them the rest (phase72);
- network B at 1280x736: 65.7 ms; the full-resolution blocks 21-22 (33 %), the bottleneck 8.8
  (13 %), the four half-resolution window blocks 6.2 (9 %) (phase72).

**The GPU's clock on Windows.** Intel's Windows driver lowers the clock in the gaps between frames.
On this laptop's host path that held B's network at 0.85 of A's instead of 0.5 (phase72, Mortal
Kombat 11). It may cost the A-series as much, but no report shows it yet: #25's frames on the GPU
path ran at the speed test's pace.

## Older figures, for the record

- #18's 1-2 fps on an A750 (10-14, 0.0.1.x) was before frames stayed on the GPU. #32's
  screenshot of 11-02 shows an A750 in Euro Truck Simulator 2, judging by its interface, at
  1920x1080 and render scale 0.6: network field 1216x704 and 154 ms a frame, as the setup
  window read it after 37 frames. The network is not named. Network A's fit on the A770, scaled by the A750's probe rate (two thirds of an
  A770's at 32 lanes), predicts about 150 ms with the passes around it; network B's, about
  85.
- #23's Hogwarts Legacy ran at 2560x1440 with XeSS Quality and frame generation x2, at 55-60
  presented fps, so 27-30 rendered. NR runs after XeSS at 2560x1440, which at 0.4 is a
  1024x576 field. On that A770 network A takes about 75 ms there, and B about 41. Either way
  27-30 frames a second is more than NR on every frame allows. Whether NR was on is asked
  (11-01) and not yet answered.
- #34's Assetto Corsa on a B580: 9-15 fps on network A at 1920x1080 ("Said"), and smooth and
  playable on B at render scale 0.5. At 0.75 the frame rate "drops quite dramatically", as the
  field grows from 0.55 to 1.2 MP (960x576 to 1472x832).
- #15's Mafia III on a B570 at 2560x1440, "approximately 30 fps", was judged by eye on 0.0.1.x.

## What would cut the time

- **On the A-series, the cost per pass is the first thing.** It is half of a frame at the
  sizes games run, and the probe's rates do not explain it: they set the part per megapixel.
  A per-pass profile on such a card, with the timestamps
  of `frame_profile.py --warm`, would show whether the time goes to the passes themselves or
  to the gaps between them. Eight A-series reporters (#22-#26, #29, #30, #32) can run test
  builds. Fewer passes would help every GPU, and the A-series most. What the probe offers
  these cards besides, 8 or 16 lanes at 1.5x the rate of 32, would act on the part per pixel,
  the smaller part here.
- **The speed test should measure the GPU path** that games use, so that the number a player
  reads is the frame NR adds. Not done.
- **A frame-time line in the in-game panel**, as #19 asked: the setup window shows the
  daemon's time, but nothing in the game does.
- On every GPU, the part per pixel is the render scale's, and the player controls it.
